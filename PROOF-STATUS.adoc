=== Canonical Agda profile (2026-11-09)

PROOF-POLICY §2.1 (standards, owner ruling 2026-11-01) makes
`agda --safe --without-K --cubical-compatible` the canonical invocation for
first-party Agda theory proofs. Every gate now adds `--cubical-compatible`:
`scripts/check.sh`, `scripts/check-siblings.sh`,
`scripts/correspondence.sh`, the Justfile recipes, the library file and the
K9 proof configuration, whose contract and targeted negative fixture now
require it. The core, reject and correspondence modules declare it in
their pragmas. The flag is coinfective: a module that declares it cannot
import one that does not. Downstream developments that adopt the profile,
such as MetaManifold-Evidence, therefore need this change first. The
integration modules keep `--safe --without-K` in their pragmas because
echo-types and epistemic-types do not declare the flag, and the sibling
gate checks them under it from the command line.

=== Partial-recovery comparison (2026-11-07)

The optional integration now imports the actual
`EpistemicTypes.PartialRecovery` interface from `epistemic-types` commit
`6ce8c74e8a91e5021cb0c1fb4fc805bdff837178` (merged PR #50). The Echo and
standard-library pins are unchanged. This addition is separate from the
hosted receipts above; it requires a fresh hosted run for its own tree.

link:tests/integration/EpistemicPartialRecovery.agda[EpistemicPartialRecovery]
uses the *joint visible/residue pair* as the residual observation. It proves:

* a proof-relevant image witness and a candidate with trivial evidence
  translate in both directions with pointwise inverse laws;
* source separation identifies every candidate of any inhabited pair case,
  including cases refined by additional evidence;
* identification of *every* inhabited pair case with trivial evidence implies
  source separation (identification of one refined case does not suffice);
* an already supplied `PartialRecovery` identifies such cases and decodes
  each candidate's pair to its source;
* adding the separate `SuccessSound` law to that decoder provides an
  `ImageDecision`, alongside identification of all inhabited pair cases;
* identification of all those cases *plus an explicit `ImageDecision`*
  constructs a partial decoder with the sibling's separate `SuccessSound`
  law. Identification alone supplies no decision or search algorithm.

`Case.witness` contains a source. Its projection supplies an identified
value after separation is proved, but is not recovery from a pair alone.
The bridge neither identifies an actual historical world without the
usual observation/evidence premises nor establishes equality of evidence
proofs or equality of functions.

Agda also reports `UnsupportedIndexedMatch` warnings in the imported
`EpistemicTypes.EchoBridge` arithmetic lemmas, in addition to the existing
standard-library warnings in the Echo comparison. No computation under
cubical transports is claimed by this integration.

link:tests/integration/EpistemicPartialRecoveryExamples.agda[The examples]
include executable recovery for an explicitly decided faithful retention,
and a retention with a singleton fibre and a colliding fibre. The latter
has a locally identified case and decidable image membership, but provably
has no global `PartialRecovery`. Two new rejection controls attempt to
project a chosen consistency witness as recovery and to promote a single
identified case to the global identification premise. Their valid prefixes
are checked before their final invalid declarations.

=== Contexts of assumptions

Evidence is a context `Ctx W = List (W → Set)`, interpreted as the product of
its assumptions, with order-preserving thinnings `Δ ⊆ Γ` between contexts.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Checked result |Evidence
|Evidence restricts along a thinning
|`Context.weaken`: a thinning `Δ ⊆ Γ` maps evidence for `Γ` to evidence for `Δ`.
|Cases and claims move in the right directions
|`Context.case-weaken` (a case under more assumptions is a case under fewer) and
`Context.claim-strengthen` (a claim under fewer assumptions holds under more,
via `Core.refine-claim`).
|Nothing thins into the empty context from nothing
|`Context.no-thinning-into-empty`: a non-empty context has no thinning into `[]`.
|===

Source: link:src/ResidualEvidence/Context.agda[Context].

=== Dependency-preserving composition

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Checked result |Evidence
|Concatenated evidence splits and rejoins exactly
|`Composition.join-split` and `Composition.split-join`: both inverse laws for
`Γ ++ Δ`; `split-is-weaken-left` and `split-is-weaken-right` identify the
projections with thinnings.
|Claims compose without inventing consistency
|`Composition.compose-claims`: the composed case is an explicit argument, never
derived from the two components; `component-left` and `component-right`
recover each component case.
|Paired observations are an ordinary fibre
|`Composition.pair-from-to` and `Composition.pair-to-from`: both inverse laws
between candidates of a paired observation and pairs of candidates.
|Coarsening transfers claims in one direction only
|`Composition.coarsen-candidate`, `coarsen-case` and `coarsen-claim`: a coarse
claim transfers to the fine observation, not the other way.
|Worked example
|`Examples.Composition.present-and-two`: `[NoiseBound] ++ [NoNoise]` yields
presence and `u ≡ 2`; `coarsening-forgets-presence`: under the coarse
"positive or not" observation the silent candidate `(0,1)` is admitted.
|===

=== Evidence revision and retraction

Refutation and disagreement are constructive witnesses, not negations of
`Holds`, so they survive `--safe` and transport along retractions.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Checked result |Evidence
|Constructive refutation implies the negative
|`Revision.refuted-not-holds` and `Revision.disagreeing-not-identified`.
|Retraction is a thinning with its own inverse laws
|`Revision.retract-⊆`, `retract-join-split`, `retract-split-join` and
`retract-is-weaken`.
|What survives a retraction
|`Revision.retract-case`, `refuted-retract`, `disagreeing-retract`;
`survives-retraction` for a claim whose support is kept, and
`dependent-holds` / `survives-dependent` for claims that rest on named assumptions.
|Revision replaces an assumption
|`Revision.replace-head` and `Revision.revise`: the revised case is an explicit
argument.
|Worked example
|`Examples.Revision.presence-survives` retracting `NoNoise`, while
`identification-lost` and `identification-not-recovered` show the value is no
longer identified; `presence-refuted` after retracting `NoiseBound`, via the
witness `(0,2)`; `balanced-identifies-one` after revision; `no-revised-case`
when the revised assumptions are inconsistent.
|===

=== Certified finite checker

The checker works over the explorer's own model: signed integers `u, n` in
−6..6, a noise bound 0..6, the three observation views (exact, sign,
magnitude) and the optional assumption that the latent is zero, giving 546
configurations. `Checker.Evidence cfg` is a three-assumption context, so the
in-range condition the explorer hides inside its loop bounds is an explicit
premise here.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Checked result |Evidence
|Integer arithmetic and the range
|`Finite.Int.range-sound` and `range-complete`: `range` is exactly −6..6.
|Admissibility is decided correctly
|`Finite.Soundness.admits-sound` and `admits-complete`.
|Enumeration is exactly the admissible in-range worlds
|`Finite.Soundness.enumerate-sound` and `enumerate-complete`.
|Every presence verdict is sound
|`entailed-sound`, `refuted-sound`, `unresolved-sound` and
`inconsistent-sound`; `entailed-complete` for the converse of entailment.
|Identification verdicts are sound
|`identified-sound`, `unidentified-sound` and `unidentified-refutes`.
|A finite verdict reaches an actual world only under all three assumptions
|`Finite.Soundness.finite-actual-sound`: observation equality, the in-range
condition, the noise bound and (if assumed) the zero latent are all explicit
premises, discharged through `Core.actual-world-sound`.
|===

== Expected rejections

`just check` first type-checks the positive modules, then requires every
module under `tests/reject` to fail at its intended declaration:

* `FalsePresence`: using the bounded theorem after dropping its evidence.
* `FalseIdentification`: claiming every bounded candidate has `u=2`.
* `InconsistentCase`: constructing a case whose witness violates the noise bound.
* `FalseWeakening`: reading a thinning backwards, which would manufacture a
  noise bound out of no assumption.
* `FalseCoarsening`: transferring a fine claim to the coarse observation, which
  also admits the silent `(0,1)`.
* `FalseRetraction`: keeping presence after retracting `NoiseBound`, the
  assumption it rests on.
* `FalseIntEquality`: deciding that −1 and 1 are equal.
* `FalseFiniteEntailment`: claiming entailment where the checker refutes
  presence (residual 2, bound 6, exact view, zero latent assumed).
* `FalseFiniteSoundness`: transferring a finite verdict to an actual world
  without the in-range premise.

The runner checks the diagnostic shape as well as the exit status. A missing
module, parse error, unresolved name or unresolved hole does not count as a
successful expected rejection. Diagnostic logs go to ignored `_build/check-logs/`.

The warrant object can exist without a token; arbitrary sibling warrants
remain non-factive. These statements do not establish factivity of every
`Epi` value.

=== Sibling rejection controls

`scripts/check-siblings.sh` discovers the Echo and Epistemic controls under
`tests/integration/reject/` and requires both families to be present:

* `FalseEchoComposition`: supplying only the left case where composition
  requires an inhabited joint case.
* `FalseEchoCoarsening`: using a fine-observation proof on a newly admitted
  coarse candidate.
* `FalseEpistemicRetraction`: reusing the old support thinning after
  `NoNoise` has been removed.
* `FalseEpistemicRevision`: reusing that support through a revision whose
  intermediate context no longer contains `NoNoise`.
* `FalseEpistemicNonVacuity`: extracting falsehood with `sound-epi` from an
  arbitrary `Warrant`, which lacks the required soundness proof.
* `FalseEpistemicActualWorld`: obtaining an actual-world conclusion without
  the actual world's required evidence.
* `FalseEpistemicWitnessRecovery`: using a chosen consistency witness as
  though it recovered another source with the same pair.
* `FalseEpistemicLocalRecovery`: using one locally identified case in place
  of identification of every inhabited pair case, even with an image decision.

The runner first checks both positive comparisons. For every control it
also checks the valid prefix before the final `invalid` declaration, then
requires a type-error diagnostic inside that declaration. A helper failure,
missing import, parse error, unresolved hole or prover crash is a gate
failure. Logs go to ignored `_build/check-logs/`. The original six controls
and their valid prefixes passed in hosted run `41671834379`; the current
inventory has eight controls and requires a fresh hosted run.

=== Reproducing the comparisons

Run the core and pinned sibling gates:

[source,bash]
----
just check
just check-siblings
# Equivalent combined core and sibling entry point:
just check-all
----

`just correspondence` remains a separate explorer gate. For an individual
positive comparison against explicitly supplied source directories:

[source,bash]
----
just check-epistemic /path/to/epistemic-types/src
just check-echo /path/to/echo-types/proofs/agda /path/to/agda-stdlib/src
----

The comparisons support treating this core as an ordinary evidence-refined
fibre with a model-relative warrant interface. They do not establish a new
primitive type or a distinct mathematical theory. The next research question
is whether dependency-preserving composition and evidence revision add a
useful interface beyond direct use of these foundations. In particular,
transporting residual operations across the interfaces does not prove that
all their laws are instances of sibling laws; evidence-context composition
is not identified here with Echo's function-composition isomorphism.
